## IC upgrades

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Aug 15, 2010|43_

> Has anyone tried upgrading the LF356 chips in M450B summing amps, or got any recommendations on good options for this, or whether it is worth doing? I use the summing mixer I built from a pair of these every day, and while I am very happy with it I was wondering whether I would gain anything from a chip upgrade.

— **rocksure**, Dec 5, 2010|43

> Hi, RockSure (I think I sold you your M450Bs, btw - glad they are working so well for you... your song on your website sounds great.)
> I had (some time ago now) tried various substitutes for the LF356 opamps, and ultimately ended up putting the stock 356s back in. I did manage to get a somewhat more "hifi" sound with one or more of the Burr-Brown modern opamps (I wish I'd kept notes to let you know which ones I tried - I have lots of opamps), and I think I also got a somewhat lower noise floor, but I basically liked the sound better with the originals. If I recall, the place in the circuit where these opamps are used (I think they are the only opamps in an otherwise all-discrete board) was as a virtual-ground (virtual-earth - not sure which term you kiwis use) summing stage, where the non-inverting input is grounded - without looking at the schemo again, I may be wrong... Anyway, I remember doing a little research on the LF356, and it turned out they were quite highly regarded by audiophiles and used in some very good equipment (among them was a Neumann EQ, IIRC), and used in some excellent A-D converters, etc. I just did a quick Google of "LF356 audio" and re-found this guy raving about it: 
> http://boneshifi.blogspot.com/2009/09/op-amp-ic-for-your-hi-fi-needs.html
> You'll see a lot of posts from folks wishing to "upgrade" various standard opamps, like 5532s, etc., but they are often dealing with some of the best-sounding equipment made, and I generally think we are better off with the design choices made by the original design engineer. That said, I have nothing against mods and upgrades, and encourage it where it makes sense, and if you know what you are doing. One thing to watch with some of the WBS (and many others) use of opamps, is the compensation caps used with some of them. They were used to tune the frequency response / bandwidth of that circuit stage. I don't believe that applies to these LF356, though, but it does apply to the frequent use of the LM201/301 chips in a lot of WBS gear, for example. A lot of folks would consider those "bad" chips, but with proper design they could obviously sound very good. Other things to watch with chip swaps are the input and output impedance matches and especially the ability to drive a low impedance load, where necessary, and then how much current or power is drawn by the new chips - I've heard of some folks overloading the power supply by using lots of chips that draw more power (even a small increase per chip can really add up in something like a mixing desk). Oh, watch the supply voltage ratings, also - I think most of the WBS applications use +-18V, and some chips aren't designed to take rails that high. All that said, feel free to grab a few appropriate chips and have at it. I still like the original LF356s though in this application, personally. Great specs often don't mean great sound; it's all about your ears. Have fun, keep making good music... cheers.

## Help restoring my WBS 1204

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Feb 15, 2012|45_

> Hello Cassidy.
>
> I presume this is your first restoration effort? It is best to start with the power-supplies - rebuild them first and clean them up. Next would be to pull all EQ's and input modules - and listen to the sub and master sections. Then take (1) M461 module and walk it across each input - terminate its input with a line level signal like an oscillator from the console or protools, and do measurements to listen to the routing and busing. Next do the same with an EQ and the same M461. Once you have determined that the ins/outs/assignments are relatively OK - then deal with each module on a 1 by 1 basis to determine who is sick and who is OK. At this stage do NOT worry about cleaning switches, etc... just get the audio to pass cleanly. I believe this board sat for a few years - so pull all the summing amplifyer cards - the M450B's and put a little WD40 on the edge connectors and re-seat them (not a bad idea for every gold connection point in the console - just a bit on a cloth to rub on and off).
>
> Ideally you will be able to determine if the console works. Then who is sick, and of whom is sick what the illness is, etc... I forget - does this board have a built in M405 If not you should buy one. It is an extended range VU meter. You can accurately measure the signal going in and out of anywhere in the console thus determining where you drop -15 db etc.. where you are high (umm audio levels please) etc... 
>
> As a last point of interest - not only should you open up each module and re-seat each daughter board on its mother board, but if this console were mine - I would replace EVERY single LM201 to a modern LM301, and I would do the same on all op-amps in the EQ as well. You will experience a very significant drop in self noise and distortion - almost bring it back to factory spec.
>
> OH and get a copy of the manual - Theo had a similar board. Ask him to whom he sold it and bug them for a photocopy. The WBSPS will have a manual for a 1204 console posted soon, but I have to get to it first!!!
>
> Good luck.
>
> Wb!
